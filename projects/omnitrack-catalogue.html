Omnitrack Catalogue

Redesigning a 32-page industrial product catalogue for print in 14 languages

Role
Graphic Designer (Editorial)
Tool
Adobe InDesign, Illustrator, Photoshop, After Effects
Timeline
2020 (3 months on-site, finished remotely)
Team
I worked as the sole designer on the catalogue, coordinating remotely with Omnitrack's team in England for content, translations, and product photography.

Overview

This project was an update and redesign of Omnitrack's product catalogue, printed in 14 different languages. Omnitrack manufactures a kind of bearing used across countless industrial applications, for light and heavy loads alike — their clients include NASA, Rolls-Royce, and several airlines.

The new catalogue has 32 pages, 8 of them entirely new (pneumatics units, the Omniwheel series, ball tables and platforms, and a page about the company). The content is dense and full of minimal technical details I needed to carefully preserve — the main challenge was making the pages feel cleaner and lighter without removing or changing any information.

I couldn't change the texts, since the catalogue was going to be printed in 14 languages and re-translating everything would have been costly, so I kept all the copy exactly as it was in the previous version. I was also asked not to add extra pages, which made it a challenge to fit everything with good legibility without the layout looking too condensed.

The catalogue is published on the company's website at omnitrack.com/pdf-catalogue.

Layout & diagramming

I redesigned every page's layout, keeping the same information but giving it more breathing room, and diagrammed the 8 brand-new pages from scratch — including the Omniwheel range, plastic castors, spring-loaded units, ball transfer rails and skates, and a page telling the company's history since 1909.

Catalogue cover

For the front and back covers, I was asked to keep the ice-skaters photo, the main products, and all the text — so I kept the same information as the old version, but made it feel less cluttered and more modern.

Technical drawings

Every technical drawing was redesigned — some needed correcting against the real products, others were brand-new products I had to diagram from scratch. I created a new flat style, avoiding strokes and gradients to keep the drawings clean and easy to understand. In total there are 40 drawings, 6 or more of them entirely new.

Tables & charts

I created a new style for every table and chart, again avoiding strokes and heavy lines and using light colours to help guide the data reading. A lot of the information in those tables had also been updated, so I had to carefully check all of it several times.

Icons

The old catalogue's icons had no defined style or colour pattern, so I redesigned them too. I adapted some from existing icon sets, and created others from scratch based on the company's products and their functionalities — more than 40 icons in total, in a flat style consistent with the technical drawings.

Product photos

I reused a lot of photos from the previous catalogue, but some new products needed brand-new photography. I photographed, edited, and manipulated images in Adobe Photoshop, and created animated GIFs in Adobe After Effects to show the movement of some products — GIFs that were later reused on the website.

I wasn't always at the company to photograph products myself — sometimes I was working remotely from Brazil, so other people photographed and sent me the images, usually at a lower quality than I needed. I edited them to look better: cutting the background, correcting colours, and sometimes changing elements in the photo. All the steel spheres, for instance, were replaced to create a consistent pattern across products.

Sometimes I needed a photo of a product we didn't have on hand to shoot — like these supports, for example — so I manipulated existing images to create a convincing preview of it for the catalogue.

Translation

The catalogue was going to be produced in 14 different languages — such a big job that, to avoid manually copying and pasting more than 2,000 terms, I suggested using the Data Merge function in Adobe InDesign.

To make it work, I built a spreadsheet with every term from the old catalogue and gave each one a variable name, which I then applied throughout the InDesign layout. I converted the spreadsheet into a CSV file and imported it as the data source with Data Merge. I then just had to manually adjust the size of the text boxes, since some languages needed more space than others for the same text.

Additional materials

Beyond the catalogue itself, I was asked to design a business card, letterhead, van livery, cardboard box, warranty card, email footer (design and HTML code), entrance totem, and the company website.

Conclusion

This was a challenging project — I'd never worked with this kind of product before, so I had to learn everything from scratch, across a massive range of products full of technical detail. It was also my first work opportunity abroad, so at first I struggled a little with the different language and culture. In the end, everything went well, and I was happy with both the result and my own professional and personal growth.

I'm from Brazil, and this project gave me the chance to work in two different countries: two months in a stunning building in Stroud, England, and one month in Milan and Cesano Maderno, Italy. I made lots of friends and travelled a lot around both countries — it was the best experience I've had, and one I still carry into every project since.
